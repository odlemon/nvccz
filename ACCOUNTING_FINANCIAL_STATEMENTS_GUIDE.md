# The Arcus Accounting Supplementary Guide

This comprehensive guide details the accounting process, distinguishing between user input interfaces (Sub-Ledgers) and the non-negotiable double-entry records (Journal Entries and the General Ledger).

---

## Phase 1: The Core Philosophy (The Rules of the Universe)

All accounting is based on the Accounting Equation:

**Assets = Liabilities + Equity**

This equation must remain balanced after every single transaction.

### The Five Core Account Types

| Account Type | Arcus CoA Range | Natural Balance | To Increase the Account | To Decrease the Account |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------------|-------------------------|
| Assets | 1000 - 1999 | Debit (Left) | Debit (DR) | Credit (CR) |
| Liabilities | 2000 - 2999 | Credit (Right) | Credit (CR) | Debit (DR) |
| Equity | 3000 - 3999 | Credit (CR) | Credit (CR) | Debit (DR) |
| Revenue | 4000 - 4999 | Credit (CR) | Credit (CR) | Debit (DR) |
| Expenses | 5000 - 5999 | Debit (DR) | Debit (DR) | Credit (CR) |

---

## Phase 2: The Transaction Engine (The Machinery)

In a modern ERP like Arcus, there is a crucial difference between the User Interface and the System's Double-Entry Record.

### 2.1 The Chart of Accounts (CoA) - The Map (Frequency: Static/Annual Review)

The CoA is the complete, numbered list of financial categories. It is defined upon system setup and reviewed annually or when a new business activity begins.

### 2.2 Sub-Ledgers (The User's Primary Input Screens)

Sub-Ledgers are specialized modules used for high-volume transactions. They provide a user-friendly interface but are not the official double-entry record.

| Sub-Ledger | Primary Function | Transactions Tracked | Frequency of Use |
|------------|------------------|---------------------|------------------|
| Cashbook | Entry point for all bank receipts and payments. | All entries affecting Asset 1100 (Bank Account). | Continuous/Daily |
| Debtors Module | Tracks all customer invoices and payments due. | All entries affecting Asset 1300 (Accounts Receivable). | Continuous/Daily |
| Creditors Module | Tracks all vendor bills and payments owed. | All entries affecting Liability 2000 (Accounts Payable). | Continuous/Daily |

### 2.3 Journal Entries (JE) - The Atomic Record (Frequency: Continuous/Instant)

The JE is the single, non-negotiable double-entry record that the system uses to maintain balance.

- **Role:** Enforces the **Debit = Credit** rule.
- **Creation:** When a user completes an entry in a Sub-Ledger (like the Cashbook), the Arcus system instantly and automatically generates the corresponding, fully balanced Journal Entry and posts it to the General Ledger.
- **Example (Cashbook Entry):** A user enters a rent payment of ZIG 2,000 into the Cashbook. The system automatically creates the following JE:

| Account Name | Account No. | Debit (DR) | Credit (CR) |
|--------------|-------------|------------|-------------|
| Rent Expense | 5XXX | 2,000 | |
| Cash/Bank Account | 1100 | | 2,000 |
| **Total** | | **2,000** | **2,000** |

---

## Phase 3: The Monthly Flow & Integrity Check

### 3.1 The General Ledger (GL) - The Master Database (Frequency: Continuous/Real-Time)

The GL is the heart of the accounting system.

- **Process:** It receives every JE from the Sub-Ledgers and the General Journal. The GL updates the running balance of every single account in the CoA.
- **Role:** It is the single, consolidated source of truth for all transactions.
- **Frequency:** It is a continuous, real-time ledger.

### 3.2 The Trial Balance (TB) - The Integrity Check (Frequency: Period-End)

The TB is the periodic snapshot of the GL.

- **Process:** The TB lists the final ending balance for every account in the GL.
- **Critical Validation:** Total Debits MUST equal Total Credits. This check is mandatory before financial statements can be published.
- **Frequency:** Generated formally at the end of the reporting period (Monthly, Quarterly, or Annually).

### 3.3 Bank Reconciliation (Frequency: Daily/Weekly)

The final step for the Cashbook.

- **Process:** This is where the balance of the Cashbook's sub-ledger is manually verified against the external Bank Statement.
- **Role:** Ensures that all cash transactions recorded in Arcus have actually cleared the bank.
- **Frequency:** Performed daily or weekly to catch errors early.

---

## Phase 4: The Final Output (The Financial Statements)

Once the Monthly Trial Balance is confirmed balanced, the four main financial reports are generated by automatically slicing the data from the TB based on the CoA range.

| Statement | Purpose | CoA Accounts Used | Frequency of Creation |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Income Statement (IS) | Shows performance (Profit/Loss) over a period of time. | Revenue (4xxx) and Expenses (5xxx). | Monthly (Standard Reporting) |
| Balance Sheet (BS) | Shows position (A=L+E) at a single point in time. | Assets (1xxx), Liabilities (2xxx), Equity (3xxx). | Monthly (Standard Reporting) |
| Cash Flow Statement (CFS) | Explains the movement of the Cash account (1100) balance during the period. | All JEs impacting Cash (1100). | Monthly |
| Statement of Equity | Explains the movement of the Equity account (3xxx). | Net Income from IS, plus contributions/less distributions. | Monthly |

## Accounting Drill-Down: Cashbook to Income Statement Flow

This section drills down into the process where user inputs in the Cashbook (the Sub-Ledger for cash transactions) flow through the General Ledger (GL) to generate the Trial Balance (TB) and the final Income Statement (IS).

### 1. Chart of Accounts (CoA) for the P&L Cycle

We will use the following specific accounts in our demonstration, focusing on the Asset and Expense/Revenue accounts directly impacted by the Cashbook.

| Account Type | CoA Range | Account No. | Account Name | Natural Balance | IS/BS Location |
|--------------|-----------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Asset | 1000 - 1999 | 1100 | Bank/Cash Account | Debit | Balance Sheet |
| Revenue | 4000 - 4999 | 4001 | Management Fees Income | Credit | Income Statement |
| Expense | 5000 - 5999 | 5110 | Office Rental Expense | Debit | Income Statement |
| Expense | 5000 - 5999 | 5120 | Utilities Expense | Debit | Income Statement |

### 2. The Transaction Flow (Cashbook to General Ledger)

We will track three separate transactions over a period, all initiated from the Cashbook Sub-Ledger.

**Scenario: The Starting Point**
- Bank Account (1100) Opening Balance: ZIG 100,000

#### Transaction 1: Paying Rent (Expense)

- **User Action:** User records a payment of ZIG 5,000 for office rent in the Cashbook.
- **System Action:** Arcus instantly generates a balanced Journal Entry (JE).

| Account No. | Account Name | DR (Increase) | CR (Decrease) |
|-------------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| 5110 | Office Rental Expense | 5,000 | |
| 1100 | Bank/Cash Account | | 5,000 |

#### Transaction 2: Receiving Management Fees (Revenue)

- **User Action:** User records a receipt of ZIG 15,000 for management fees in the Cashbook.
- **System Action:** Arcus instantly generates a balanced Journal Entry (JE).

| Account No. | Account Name | DR (Increase) | CR (Decrease) |
|-------------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| 1100 | Bank/Cash Account | 15,000 | |
| 4001 | Management Fees Income | | 15,000 |

#### Transaction 3: Paying Utilities (Expense)

- **User Action:** User records a payment of ZIG 1,500 for the water and electricity bill in the Cashbook.
- **System Action:** Arcus instantly generates a balanced Journal Entry (JE).

| Account No. | Account Name | DR (Increase) | CR (Decrease) |
|-------------|--------------|---------------|---------------|
| 5120 | Utilities Expense | 1,500 | |
| 1100 | Bank/Cash Account | | 1,500 |

### 3. The Integrity Check (Trial Balance)

At month-end, the General Ledger (GL) calculates the final balance for every single account by summarizing all the Debits and Credits posted. This summary is the Trial Balance (TB).

#### Calculation of Final Account Balances

1. **Bank Account (1100) (Asset):**
   - Starting Debit: 100,000
   - JE 1 Credit (Rent): (5,000)
   - JE 2 Debit (Fees): 15,000
   - JE 3 Credit (Utilities): (1,500)
   - **Final Debit Balance: ZIG 108,500**

2. **Management Fees (4001) (Revenue):**
   - JE 2 Credit: 15,000
   - **Final Credit Balance: ZIG 15,000**

3. **Office Rental (5110) (Expense):**
   - JE 1 Debit: 5,000
   - **Final Debit Balance: ZIG 5,000**

4. **Utilities Expense (5120) (Expense):**
   - JE 3 Debit: 1,500
   - **Final Debit Balance: ZIG 1,500**

#### The Trial Balance Report

| Account No. | Account Name | TB Debit Balance | TB Credit Balance |
|-------------|--------------|------------------|-------------------|
| 1100 | Bank/Cash Account | 108,500 | |
| 4001 | Management Fees Income | | 15,000 |
| 5110 | Office Rental Expense | 5,000 | |
| 5120 | Utilities Expense | 1,500 | |
| (Assume other BS accounts balance to 14,000) | (e.g., Equity/Liabilities) | | 100,000 |
| **TOTAL** | | **ZIG 115,000** | **ZIG 115,000** |

**Total DEBITS = Total CREDITS**

The Trial Balance is balanced, proving the integrity of all Journal Entries posted from the Cashbook.

### 4. The Financial Output (Income Statement)

The balanced Trial Balance is then used to generate the Income Statement (IS). The system automatically pulls only the accounts in the Revenue (4xxx) and Expense (5xxx) ranges.

**Net Income = Total Revenue - Total Expenses**

#### Income Statement Report (P&L)

| Item | Account No. | TB Balance |
|------|-------------|------------|
| **REVENUE** | | |
| Management Fees Income | 4001 | 15,000 |
| **TOTAL REVENUE** | | **15,000** |
| **LESS: EXPENSES** | | |
| Office Rental Expense | 5110 | (5,000) |
| Utilities Expense | 5120 | (1,500) |
| **TOTAL EXPENSES** | | **(6,500)** |
| **NET INCOME (Profit)** | | **ZIG 8,500** |

This Net Income of ZIG 8,500 is the final result of the Cashbook activity and is the key figure that will later be transferred to the Statement of Changes in Equity (and thus the Balance Sheet).

## Accounting Drill-Down: Cashbook to Cash Flow Statement Flow (CFS)

The Cash Flow Statement (CFS) is unique because it is the only statement that focuses only on the Cash Account (1100). It takes the net cash change and categorizes it into three activities: Operating, Investing, and Financing.

### 1. Relevant Chart of Accounts (CoA)

We are tracking the change in the Asset account 1100 (Bank/Cash) and categorizing the entries in the Expense/Revenue accounts (4xxx and 5xxx) that caused this change.

| Account Type | CoA Range | Account No. | Account Name | CFS Activity Classification | Reason |
|--------------|-----------|-------------|--------------|----------------------------|---------|
| Asset | 1000 - 1999 | 1100 | Bank/Cash Account | N/A (The Focus) | All Cash flows are measured against the movement in this account. |
| Revenue | 4000 - 4999 | 4001 | Management Fees Income | Operating | Day-to-day business activity that generates revenue. |
| Expense | 5000 - 5999 | 5110 | Office Rental Expense | Operating | Day-to-day cost of running the business. |
| Expense | 5000 - 5999 | 5120 | Utilities Expense | Operating | Day-to-day cost of running the business. |
| Asset | 1000 - 1999 | 1500 | Investment in Subsidiary | Investing | Buying or selling long-term assets or investments. |

**Note:** The CFS is typically prepared using the Indirect Method for the Operating Activities section, but since all our sample transactions were cash-based, the cash flows from operating activities will equal the Net Income.

### 2. The General Ledger (GL) Movement and Net Cash Change

We use the same transactions as the previous example. Every transaction recorded in the Cashbook becomes a Journal Entry (JE) and hits the General Ledger (GL), specifically affecting the Bank Account (1100).

| Transaction | JE DR/CR | Impact on Bank/Cash (1100) | Amount (ZIG) | Corresponding Account | CFS Activity |
|-------------|----------|---------------------------|--------------|----------------------|--------------|
| Starting Balance | N/A | | 100,000 | N/A | N/A |
| T1: Pay Rent | CR (Cash Outflow) | | (5,000) | Office Rental Expense (5110) | Operating |
| T2: Receive Fees | DR (Cash Inflow) | | 15,000 | Management Fees Income (4001) | Operating |
| T3: Pay Utilities | CR (Cash Outflow) | | (1,500) | Utilities Expense (5120) | Operating |
| Ending Balance | | Net Change | 8,500 | | |

- **Final Bank/Cash Balance:** ZIG 100,000 (Start) + ZIG 8,500 (Net Change) = ZIG 108,500

### 3. The Cash Flow Statement (CFS)

The CFS takes the total cash change (ZIG 8,500) and organizes it. Since all our transactions were part of the core business function, they all fall under Operating Activities.

#### A. Cash Flows from Operating Activities

This section tracks cash generated or used by the main business activities (Revenue and Expenses).

| Description | Cash Flow |
|-------------|-----------|
| Cash received from customers (Management Fees) | 15,000 |
| Cash paid for rent (Office Rental Expense) | (5,000) |
| Cash paid for utilities (Utilities Expense) | (1,500) |
| **Net Cash from Operating Activities** | **8,500** |

#### B. Cash Flows from Investing Activities

This section tracks cash used to buy or sell long-term assets (Property, Plant, Equipment, or Investments).

- Since we had no transactions involving buying new equipment or making long-term investments (e.g., investing ZIG 50,000 and debiting Asset 1500), this section is zero.

| Description | Cash Flow |
|-------------|-----------|
| Purchases of Property, Plant, & Equipment | 0 |
| Sales of Investments | 0 |
| **Net Cash from Investing Activities** | **0** |

#### C. Cash Flows from Financing Activities

This section tracks cash related to owners (Equity) or lenders (Liabilities), such as issuing/repaying loans or paying dividends.

- Since we had no transactions involving bank loans, receiving capital from owners, or paying dividends, this section is zero.

| Description | Cash Flow |
|-------------|-----------|
| Proceeds from Loans | 0 |
| Payments of Dividends | 0 |
| **Net Cash from Financing Activities** | **0** |

#### D. Final Reconciliation

The final section reconciles the total cash change back to the General Ledger's opening and closing balances for the Cash Account (1100).

| Summary Item | Amount (ZIG) |
|--------------|--------------|
| Net Increase in Cash (A+B+C) | 8,500 |
| Plus: Cash at beginning of period | 100,000 |
| **Cash at end of period** | **108,500** |

### Summary of the Cash Flow Purpose

The Cash Flow Statement proves two critical things for the Arcus system:

1. **Integrity Check:** The final Cash at end of period (ZIG 108,500) must perfectly match the final Bank/Cash Account (1100) balance in the Trial Balance. If they don't match, the system has a data integrity error.

2. **Narrative:** It tells the story of where the ZIG 8,500 increase came from (in this case, solely from operations), which provides a more granular view than just seeing the final balance.

This flow confirms how every cash transaction instantly generates a Journal Entry, which simultaneously updates the GL balance (for the TB) and feeds into the category buckets for the Cash Flow Statement.

## Accounting Drill-Down: Cashbook to Balance Sheet Flow

The Balance Sheet is a snapshot of the company's financial health at a single point in time. It only lists the closing balances of permanent accounts (Assets, Liabilities, and Equity).

### 1. Updated Chart of Accounts (CoA)

We include all accounts from the previous examples, plus the central Equity account where Net Income is transferred.

| Equity | 3000 - 3999 | 3000 | Owner's/Shareholder's Equity | Credit | Balance Sheet |
| Revenue | 4000 - 4999 | 4001 | Management Fees Income | Credit | Income Statement (Closes to Equity) |

| Expense | 5000 - 5999 | 5120 | Utilities Expense | Debit | Income Statement (Closes to Equity) |

### 2. General Ledger (GL) Impact and Final Balances

The Net Income of ZIG 13,500 (ZIG 25,000 Revenue - ZIG 11,500 Expenses) is then transferred (closed) to the Owner's Equity (3000) account.
